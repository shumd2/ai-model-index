## Overview

**Microsoft-Decision-1** (released October 9, 2026) is a decision-scoring model on Azure AI Foundry, post-trained from **Qwen3.5-9B**. It answers structured questions — yes/no, multiple choice, rating, rubric grading — and emits a calibrated probability rather than prose.

Its pricing is the headline: **$0.042 per million input tokens, with output billed at zero**. You pay for the prompt and nothing for the verdict.

## Why free output is the point

For a classifier, output tokens are a liability, not a feature. You cannot cap a cost model when the model might ramble. Billing output at zero means the only way your bill moves is by sending more input — which is the number you actually control.

At $0.042 per million input tokens, Microsoft is competing with the small-model tier on price while offering something the general models do not: a guarantee that the response is a valid decision, not a paragraph that needs parsing.

## Part of a wider pattern

Microsoft-Decision-1 is one of three independent releases in the same week that treat "answer this decision" as a distinct model class:

| Lab | Model | Date | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liquid AI | d1-3B / d1-omni-600M | Oct 7 | Self-host | n/a (no generation) |
| Celeris | Celeris-1-decision | Oct 8 | $0.04 / 1M | Free |
| Microsoft | Microsoft-Decision-1 | Oct 9 | $0.042 / 1M | Free |

Two labs building with a diffusion-based or single-pass approach and one post-training a 9B model are converging on the same conclusion: most production traffic is decision traffic.

## Caveats

- **Not a general-purpose model.** It will not answer your questions.
- **Context window and max output are unpublished**, as are cache and batch rates.
- **Azure Foundry only** — no weights, and no fine-tuning offered in this release.
