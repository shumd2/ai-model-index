## Overview

**Liquid AI's d1 models** (released October 7, 2026) break from the language-model template entirely. They produce **no tokens at all** — a single forward pass returns the answer directly.

That is the whole design. For classification, routing and triage, sampling a hundred tokens and parsing them is wasted work. d1 collapses that into one pass.

## The two models

| Model | Inputs | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| **`LiquidAI/d1-3b`** | Text + image | Merged from an LFM2.5-2.6B text backbone and an LFM2.5-VL-3B vision backbone |
| **`LiquidAI/d1-omni-600M`** | Text + image, or text + audio | LFM2.5-Encoder-350M with FastConformer audio and vision encoders |

Both ship as **open weights** with no restrictions on download, fine-tuning or deployment, and both have **llama.cpp support from day one**.

## Why it matters

The 600M variant is small enough to run on-device. A phone or laptop can make routing decisions locally without a network round-trip — which changes the privacy calculus for anything touching personal data.

The architectural trade is straightforward: you give up the open-ended generation that makes LLMs useful for writing and reasoning, and you get latency and cost that a sampling loop cannot approach. Liquid's own testing went up to a 3.4K-token state.

## The same idea, elsewhere

d1 is not alone in this direction. Microsoft shipped **Microsoft-Decision-1** on October 9 (post-trained from Qwen3.5-9B, $0.042 per 1M input with **free output**), and Celeris released **Celeris-1-decision** on October 8 ($0.04 per 1M input, free output). All three labs independently converged on the same observation: a large share of production traffic never needed prose in the first place.

## What is unknown

- **No published token context window.** Liquid tested with state up to 3.4K tokens; that is a test ceiling, not a specification.
- **The specific licence name is not confirmed** in Liquid's announcement, though it states the weights can be used without restrictions.
- No hosted API at launch, and no standard benchmark results.
