OpenAI's 2026 DevDay delivered the fastest flagship refresh on record: **GPT-6.1 Sol, announced September 29, replaces GPT-6 Sol just seven days after that model launched.** It scores **AA 52 at max effort** — one point below GPT-6 Astra — at $2/$10 per 1M tokens, a fifth of Astra's $10/$50, with cache reads now 95% off ($0.10/M). That combination puts its max-effort cost per task at **$0.72 versus $3.26 for Astra**.

The agentic gains are the substance: +4 points on AA-Briefcase v1.1, +5 on GDPval-AA v2.1, +12 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, and AA-Omniscience accuracy up with hallucination falling from 60% to 54%. OpenAI also reported that DeepSWE v1.1 matches GPT-6 Astra at roughly one-fifth the cost, and that max-effort GDP.pdf output beats Opus 5.5 with fallbacks at under half the cost per task. Reasoning effort runs low/medium/high/xhigh/max — no `none` or `minimal` — and the model supports US and EU data residency (fast mode is unavailable with EU residency).

Around the model, OpenAI announced **"dots"** — always-on Astra-powered agents with their own cloud computer and 4,000+ app integrations — an **Ultrafast tier** for Codex (~8× faster, ~300 tok/s at roughly 6× price), a new **Pro 500 plan** at $500/month, and ChatGPT passing **1.2 billion weekly users**. GPT-6.1 Sol reached general availability on Microsoft Foundry the same day.

Notably absent: the planned **GPT-6.1 Astra** launch was shelved — see our separate item — making Sol the default recommendation for anyone weighing Astra-class quality against cost.
