Artificial Analysis and Harvey published **LAB-AA v1.1** on October 8 — a benchmark for legal work whose defining feature is that a correct answer with a fabricated citation counts as a failure.

## Hallucination-Gated All-Pass Rate

| # | Model | All-pass rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grok 4.7 (xhigh) | **9.4%** |
| 2 | Muse Spark 1.3 (max) | 8.9% |
| 3 | GPT-6 Astra (max) | 8.6% |
| 4 | GPT-6.1 Sol (max) | 6.9% |
| 5 | Claude Fable 5.1 (max, w/ fallback) | 6.4% |
| 6 | Kimi K3 (max) | 5.3% |
| 7 | Claude Opus 5.5 (max, w/ fallback) | 4.2% |

The gate is what makes this different: a result only counts if **no material hallucination appears anywhere** in it. On 120 private legal tasks, **more than 60% of otherwise-passing answers contained one**.

## Why the ranking inverts

Without the hallucination gate, **Muse Spark 1.3 would lead at 26.7%** all-pass. With it, the ordering reshuffles:

- GPT-6 Astra: 8.9% → 8.6%, moving #10 → #3
- GPT-6.1 Sol: 7.5% → 6.9%

The models that produce the most answers containing *something* fabricated are the ones that gain most from the gate. Claude Opus 5.5 sits last on this board while leading the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — the two measure genuinely different properties.

## What it measures

120 private legal tasks, gated on material hallucination rather than correctness alone. It is a specialised domain benchmark, not a general capability measure, and it is versioned separately from LAB-AA v1.0 — v1.1 figures are **not comparable** with v1.0.
