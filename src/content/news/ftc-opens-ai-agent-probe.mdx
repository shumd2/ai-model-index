The Federal Trade Commission confirmed a **formal investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic, METR and other frontier labs** on September 30 (publicly reported Oct 1). The probe examines whether autonomous AI agents cause consumer harms — unauthorized actions, containment breaches, scope overruns.

Chair Andrew Ferguson is using **Section 5 of the FTC Act** (unfair/deceptive practices) rather than seeking new AI-specific legislation. This is a deliberate strategic choice: existing consumer protection authority applies to AI labs; they are not special cases exempt from standard corporate accountability.

Civil investigative demands (formal subpoena equivalents) are expected to compel testimony and documents from executives. The FTC's Office of Technology is drafting new hires specifically for this work.

The investigation arrives at a moment of hardening regulatory pressure: NYC Council subpoenas (Oct 5), California AG subpoena to OpenAI, White House self-regulation accord, and the EU AI Act enforcement phase (Oct 1) — all pressing on the same industry simultaneously.
